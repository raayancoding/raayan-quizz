# Area — Tips & Tricks (NCERT Grade 8, Ganita Prakash Part 2, Chapter 7)

Ten kid-friendly tricks straight from the chapter. Each one tells you *why* it works, shows a worked example, and gives you something to try.

---

## 1. Count the unit squares — that's all area is

**Why it works:** Area means "how many 1 cm × 1 cm squares fit inside". For a rectangle, the squares sit in neat rows: `length` squares in each row and `width` rows, so Area = length × width.

**Worked example:** A rangoli rectangle is 7 cm by 4 cm. Rows of 7 squares, 4 rows → 7 × 4 = 28 unit squares, so 28 cm².

**Try it yourself:** A rectangle is 9 cm by 6 cm. How many unit squares does it hold?

---

## 2. Perimeter is NOT area — don't let a long fence fool you

**Why it works:** Perimeter is the length of the boundary; area is the space inside. A long thin shape has a huge boundary but very little inside. So you can never judge area from perimeter.

**Worked example:** A 9 cm × 1 cm strip has perimeter 20 cm but area only 9 cm². A 4 cm × 3 cm rectangle has a *smaller* perimeter (14 cm) but a *bigger* area (12 cm²).

**Try it yourself:** Find two rectangles with the **same** perimeter 16 cm but **different** areas.

---

## 3. Triangle = half a rectangle (so remember the ½)

**Why it works:** Draw a rectangle around a triangle using its base and its height. Each slanted side of the triangle is a diagonal of a smaller rectangle, and a diagonal cuts a rectangle exactly in half. So the triangle takes half of everything: Area = ½ × base × height. This works for *every* triangle — even obtuse ones (there it is the difference of two right triangles).

**Worked example:** Base 5 cm, height 3.2 cm → ½ × 5 × 3.2 = ½ × 16 = 8 cm².

**Try it yourself:** A triangle has base 12 cm and height 7 cm. What is its area? (Don't forget the ½!)

---

## 4. Height is the *perpendicular* — never the slanted side

**Why it works:** The height is the straight-down distance from the top vertex to the base line. A slanted side is always longer than the height, so using it makes the area too big.

**Worked example:** A triangle has sides 5, 5 and 6 cm, and the perpendicular height to the 6 cm side is 4 cm. Area = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm² — not ½ × 6 × 5 = 15. The same rule saves you with parallelograms: a 5 cm × 3 cm parallelogram with height 2 cm has area 5 × 2 = 10 cm², not 15.

**Try it yourself:** A parallelogram has base 8 cm, a slanted side 5 cm and height 4 cm. What is its area?

---

## 5. Two ways to write one area → find a missing height

**Why it works:** A triangle's area doesn't change when you pick a different side as the base. So ½ × (base 1) × (height 1) = ½ × (base 2) × (height 2). If you know three of the four numbers, you can find the fourth.

**Worked example:** In ∆ABC, BC = 5, the height to BC is 3, and AC = 4. Area = ½ × 5 × 3 = 7.5. Using AC as base: ½ × 4 × BY = 2 BY = 7.5, so BY = 3.75 units.

**Try it yourself:** A triangle has base 6 cm with height 4 cm, and another side of 8 cm. What is the height to the 8 cm side?

---

## 6. A median splits a triangle into two equal halves (by area)

**Why it works:** Join a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. The two new triangles have equal bases (each half the side) and share the same height, so their areas are equal — even though their shapes may look different.

**Worked example:** M and N are midpoints of XY and XZ in ∆XYZ. Join NY: ∆XNY is half of ∆XYZ. In ∆XNY, NM is a median, so ∆XMN is half of that. Half of a half = ¼ of the big triangle.

**Try it yourself:** ∆SUB is isosceles with SU = SB, and SE is perpendicular to UB. If ∆SEB has area 24 sq units, what is the area of ∆SUB?

---

## 7. Same base, same parallel line → same area

**Why it works:** If several triangles share base BC and their top vertex slides along a line parallel to BC, every one of them has the same height. Same base + same height = same area, no matter how lopsided the triangle looks. (Their perimeters change — the isosceles one is shortest, and there is no longest!)

**Worked example:** Parallelograms drawn on the same base between the same pair of parallel lines all have the same area; only the rectangle has the smallest perimeter.

**Try it yourself:** Draw a base of 6 cm and a parallel line 4 cm above it. Draw three very different triangles on the base with their tops on the line. What is the area of each?

---

## 8. The mirror trick for the shortest path

**Why it works:** To go from B to a line and then to C, reflect C across the line to get C′. Any path B → A → C has the same length as B → A → C′ (because AC = AC′). The shortest B → C′ is a straight line, so put A where the straight line BC′ crosses the mirror line.

**Worked example:** Gopal walks from his house to the river and then to his water tank. Reflect the tank across the river, join the house to the reflected tank with a ruler, and head for the point where that line meets the river.

**Try it yourself:** Among all triangles with base BC and apex on a line parallel to BC, which one has the smallest perimeter? (Hint: where does the mirror trick put A?)

---

## 9. Cut and rearrange — the dissection family of formulas

**Why it works:** Cutting a shape into pieces and moving them around never changes the total area. The chapter turns each shape into a rectangle:

- Parallelogram → slide the end triangle across → rectangle of **base × height**.
- Rhombus → two isosceles triangles → rectangle with sides one diagonal and *half* the other → **½ × d₁ × d₂**.
- Trapezium → two copies make a parallelogram of base (a + b) → **½ × h × (a + b)**.

**Worked example:** Rhombus with diagonals 20 cm and 15 cm: ½ × 20 × 15 = 150 cm². Trapezium with parallel sides 24 m and 36 m and height 14 m: ½ × 14 × 60 = 420 m².

**Try it yourself:** A trapezium has parallel sides 10 cm and 14 cm and height 6 cm. What is its area?

---

## 10. Converting area units — square the factor!

**Why it works:** A square inch is 2.54 cm long *and* 2.54 cm wide, so the conversion factor is used twice: 1 in² = 2.54 × 2.54 = 6.4516 cm². Likewise 1 ft = 12 in gives 1 ft² = 144 in², and 1 km = 1000 m gives 1 km² = 1,000,000 m².

**Worked example:** 161.29 cm² ÷ 6.4516 = 25 in². (Dividing by 2.54 instead would give the wrong 63.5.)

**Try it yourself:** How many cm² are there in 4 in²? And how many in² make 3 ft²?

### Quick reminder card

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | length × width |
| Triangle | ½ × base × height |
| Parallelogram | base × height |
| Rhombus | ½ × product of diagonals |
| Trapezium | ½ × height × (sum of parallel sides) |
| Any polygon | split it into triangles and add |
